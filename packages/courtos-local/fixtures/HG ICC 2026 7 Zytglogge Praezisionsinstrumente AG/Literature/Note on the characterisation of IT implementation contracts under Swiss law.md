## 1. The characterisation question

A contract for the implementation of enterprise software, such as an ERP system, does not fit neatly into either of the two classical service categories of the Swiss Code of Obligations. Where the contractor promises a defined, testable result - a system that passes agreed acceptance criteria - the agreement is naturally characterised as a contract for work within the meaning of Article 363 et seq. CO, notwithstanding that the 'work' produced is intangible. Where, instead, the contractor's obligation is better described as the careful exercise of professional skill without a guaranteed result - for example, advisory or configuration support billed on a time basis - the mandate rules of Article 394 et seq. CO are the more natural fit.

## 2. Mixed agreements

In practice, a single master services agreement for an ERP implementation commonly combines both elements: a fixed-price, milestone-based core scope (pointing towards a contract for work) together with time-and-materials change requests or advisory support (pointing towards a mandate). Swiss doctrine generally resolves this by characterising each severable obligation according to its own content, rather than forcing the entire agreement into a single box, while recognising that the parties' remedies for a failure affecting the delivery of the system as a whole will usually be governed by the contract-for-work regime where that is the dominant characterisation of the agreement.

## 3. Practical consequence for remedies

The characterisation matters most at the point of termination. Under the contract-for-work regime, a customer facing an uncured, use-preventing defect may withdraw under Article 366 CO in conjunction with Article 107 CO, provided a reasonable period to cure has first been granted. Under the mandate regime, either party may terminate at any time under Article 404 CO, subject only to a damages claim where termination occurs at an inopportune moment. The two regimes can produce materially different outcomes on the same facts, which is why the initial characterisation of the agreement - and of any severable work package within it - is so often contested in practice.
